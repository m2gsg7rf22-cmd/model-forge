## Literature and distinction from existing methods

[Shi/Fu,section3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06631v1) already describes finite-difference/rounding perturbations away from the ideal Hessian atom span and uses a rank2m estimate under known width. This draft does not present rank truncation or that warning as a new discovery. Its known-width candidate count is not a complete-recovery certificate. The paper's isolated-block access assumption still does not supply internal responses from final model outputs.

[Gavish/Donoho,sectionsII/VI](https://arxiv.org/html/1305.5870v3) studies adaptive singular-value hard thresholding under specific asymptotic low-rank plus independent white-noise models. Our deterministic stencil truncation and shared-output rounding have not been shown to satisfy those assumptions. Its thresholds can motivate a labeled heuristic/control, but its optimality/error guarantees cannot be transferred to these GLU observations without evidence.

## Research question, not a success claim

A very small relative Hessian error need not yield the correct numerical rank or an informative rank-one membership score at a machine-like relative cutoff. If the audited results confirm this issue, the next useful question is whether observation-only, noise-aware rank selection can recover a stable useful subspace WITHOUT privileged width/truth, and fail explicitly when the membership domain is intrinsically uninformative. This must eventually improve a real reconstruction route, not end with attractive spectra.

Do not fix this by changing the frozen1e-10 diagnostic criterion after results. Existing diagnostics/candidates/consumed941027..941033 remain immutable. Any new rule is a DISTINCT exploratory analysis followed by a separate preregistered fresh-owned test. A rank-selection experiment alone cannot establish gates, full map or original weights.
