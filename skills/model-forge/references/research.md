# Research leads checked 2026-10-24

These are primary sources, not reproduced success certificates. Preserve the user's full reconstruction objective while tracking the gap between each result and that objective.

- [Shi and Fu, antipodal GLU extraction (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06631): separates observations from an isolated bias-free GLU into curvature and paired-input stages. It reports recovery experiments under specific precision/access settings, without complete stored-weight recovery in the reported finite-precision cases. Final-model-output-to-block-response reduction remains open. ModelForge implements the even quadratic primitive and conditional orientation step, not the complete paper pipeline. Next steps: query-only direction candidates, magnitude/coupling recovery, conditioning/precision studies, and integration with the existing truth-free fitting engine.
- [Sarangi et al., Finite Probes Suffice (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33901): analytic-family functional identification under generic continuous probes is conditional on the model class and exact observation setting. It is not a practical optimizer, an arbitrary text-API recovery procedure, or proof of original-weight uniqueness. Hidden-probe guarantees have additional activation/architecture assumptions. Investigate constructive solvers and finite-precision conditioning separately.
- [Carlini et al., ICML2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/carlini24a.html): extracts part of a language model under specified output access, with coordinate ambiguity. Do not turn output-layer reconstruction into a full-backbone claim.

Do not copy downloaded code without checking its license. Do not fetch model weights, query paid/provider endpoints, or train on provider outputs merely because a paper used them. Preregister fresh owned teachers before fitting; keep private truth out of learner arguments, exports and Git. Distinguish new software implementation from genuinely new scientific methods; this route applies published algebra and does not claim to invent it.
